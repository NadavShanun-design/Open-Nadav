# Ending the Plateau: A Manifesto for downloading all 8 billion brains for AI to train on

By Nadav Shanun | Published 2026-01-23 on Substack | [Original post](https://nadavshanun.substack.com/p/ending-the-plateau-a-manifesto-for)

Nadav Shanun

<https://www.linkedin.com/in/nadav-shanun-742232290/>

For a decade we fed the web to machines. They learned to summarise, imitate style, and conjure fluent answers. And yet, something stubborn remains out of reach: *how people actually do things*. The living, tacit know-how that sits in our hands, our eyes, our pauses - the choreography of expertise as it unfolds on a screen or in a conversation. The internet holds many *answers*; humans hold the *process*.

Our proposal is simple, almost embarrassingly so: if today's models feel capped, it's because we've trained them on the world's *documents* rather than the world's *doing*. To move beyond the plateau, we must translate human practice into machine-readable form - ethically, voluntarily, and with precision.

## **The Missing Dataset**

There are two kinds of knowledge:

  - **Declarative** ("what is true"): articles, manuals, forum posts.

  - **Procedural** ("how it's done"): the sequence of clicks a SolidWorks veteran uses without thinking; the way a clinician frames a question; the pattern a salesperson follows to move from rapport to next steps.

We've exhausted the first. The second is scarcely digitised. It lives in 8 billion minds and is expressed through speech, attention, and action. If we could capture that *safely and consensually*, models would stop merely *talking about* tasks and start *performing* them.

## **The Instrument: An On-Screen & In-Call Copilot**

Enter a free agent that lives where we work: in the browser and in our video calls.

  - **On screen**, it sees the page structure, recognises UI elements, and can explain "what is this?" or "how do I do that?" with step-by-step guidance.

  - **In calls**, it prepares context - who you're meeting, prior notes, likely objectives - and surfaces helpful prompts as you speak.

Crucially, with explicit opt-in, it can *observe* the flow of expertise:

  - **Speech -\> text** in professional conversations (with on-device redaction of personal identifiers and sensitive details).

  - **Action -\> trace** in software (DOM states, cursor paths, command sequences, timing), capturing *how* a task is actually completed.

These observations are turned into **de-identified, structured "episodes"** of practice. Not gossip. Not surveillance. *Craft knowledge, carefully bottled*.

## **From Episodes to Agency**

Once you have clean, labelled traces of real work, several doors open:

1.  **Interface mastery**: Agents trained on click-level sequences can navigate unfamiliar software by reading the screen and acting, not guessing.

2.  **Skill transfer**: A novice gains a tutor distilled from thousands of expert episodes; the craft's muscle memory becomes teachable.

3.  **Evaluation that matters**: Benchmarks evolve from trivia to "can the model complete a CAD loft correctly in five steps?"

4.  **Generalisation**: Patterns in process - plan, act, check - become reusable across tools and domains.

This is how we replace performance that is *linguistically impressive* with performance that is *operationally reliable*.

## **The Social Contract (Non-Negotiable)**

Power without legitimacy corrodes. So the product's economics and ethics must align with users, not merely extract from them.

  - **Consent first**: Opt-in by domain, by app, by meeting. Clear controls, a kill-switch, and visibility into what's collected.

  - **Minimise and protect**: On-device filtering, PII/PHI redaction, aggregation, and differential privacy before any data leaves the machine.

  - **Right to delete**: Your contribution is revocable; your copy is portable.

  - **Contexts that require duty of care**: Healthcare, finance, and other regulated domains operate under stricter rules (e.g., HIPAA, BAAs, audit trails). Enterprise deployments can run **zero-telemetry** by design.

  - **A fair economy**: Keep it free for individuals; fund it by licensing *anonymised workflow datasets and evaluation suites* to model builders and software vendors. Optionally, offer a contributor revenue share - a data co-op rather than a data mine.

This isn't surveillance capitalism in a smarter frock. It's a voluntary *knowledge commons* - a way to publish the choreography of expertise without publishing the people.

## **Why This Ends the Stagnation**

Today's plateau isn't about bigger networks or cleverer prompts; it's about **the wrong substrate**. We've strained text to do the work of *demonstration*. But expertise is a movie, not a paragraph. By turning live practice into teachable sequences, we give models the missing reagent: *procedural grounding*. That's what will let an agent not only describe "how to book a flight" but actually complete it safely, step by step, under supervision.

## **Objections, Answered Briefly**

  - **"Isn't this risky?"** Only if done carelessly. The architecture must assume the burden: strict consent, local redaction, legal compliance, transparent governance.

  - **"Won't this homogenise craft?"** Quite the opposite. A large, diverse corpus reveals multiple viable paths, allowing agents to adapt to context, constraints, and preference.

  - **"Can't we simulate this data?"** Synthetic traces miss human pacing, error correction, and improvisation - the very signals that make expertise robust.

## **A New Intelligence Dividend**

The internet expanded access to information. This expands access to *capability*. Imagine an apprentice engineer in Lagos learning the rhythm of world-class CAD work; a rural clinician gaining bedside patterns from thousands of consenting experts; a small business owner receiving reliable, on-screen help that reflects how professionals truly operate.

That's not just better AI. That's a quieter kind of progress: a world where know-how is less gated by proximity and more available by design.

## **The Line We Do Not Cross**

There is no future worth having that treats people as mere data sources. The gift here is voluntary contribution - *humans teaching machines how to help humans better*. If we can't honour that, we don't deserve the capability.

**Thesis:** Models plateaued because we trained them on what humanity *wrote*. They'll advance when we train them on what humanity *does* - with consent, with care, and with purpose. A free copilot that learns from de-identified traces of real work is the bridge. Cross it carefully, and we step into a more intelligent, more useful future.

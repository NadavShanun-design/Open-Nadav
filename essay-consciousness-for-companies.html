Consciousness for Companies

By Nadav Shanun

Every company that intends to last has to answer one question honestly, and usually earlier than it wants to. What makes you impossible to copy?

For us, the answer comes down to three points of defensibility. These are the things that create a real technological moat:

  1. Build technology that is 10x better than anything a potential competitor could put in front of the same problem.

  2. Create a monopoly for ourselves in a specific market.

  3. Give ourselves genuine defensibility and scalability for the future.

Those three are the frame. What follows is the thing we are actually building inside that frame.

The goal

We aim to create consciousness for companies.

A company is the first rung, and it is the smallest one. The actual thesis is that we keep raising the granularity. Offices, then facilities, then ecosystems, then whole cities, then the planet. Each level is the same three-part problem at a larger scale, and each one becomes buildable only because the level under it already works. That progression is the point of the whole company, and everything in this piece is written in service of it.

We start with companies for a practical reason. They are extremely rich in data, and the data they hold is very high quality. A company produces a continuous, structured, intentional record of decisions, communication, failures, revenue, and behavior. Very few systems in the world generate that density of signal about themselves. If you want to give something a mind, you begin where the raw material is best.

What we actually mean by consciousness

The word gets used carelessly, so it is worth being precise about how we define it.

Start with the thing most people skip. Consciousness is a human-made construct. There is no measurement for it, no instrument that detects it, no agreed physical boundary where it begins. What exists is the general consensus of humankind on what the word means, accumulated over a few thousand years of people describing their own inner experience to each other and finding that the descriptions broadly match. That consensus is the entire definition. There is nothing underneath it.

That is a problem if you want to build something physical that resembles the construct, because you cannot engineer against a consensus. You can only engineer against a specification.

So we treat the definition of consciousness as the most infinite compartmentalization of definitions of what consciousness is. You take the human-made construct, the one defined by the general consensus of humankind, and you break it down into infinitely small logical axioms of truth. The test for each axiom is simple. Would every human agree with this? Realistically, would 99.9 percent of people agree with this, without needing to know anything about neuroscience or philosophy first?

Most of what people say about consciousness fails that test immediately. Free will fails it. The soul fails it. Whether animals have it fails it. Keep breaking the construct down past all of the parts people argue about, and you are left with a small number of statements that almost nobody would dispute.

There are three:

  1. Any form of consciousness has something that collects some form of information or data from the outside world. It takes in existence. Sight, sound, touch, instrumentation, whatever the mechanism happens to be. Something is coming in.

  2. Any form of consciousness has some type of process that manipulates that data or information. In our case, the human brain. It orders what came in, connects it to what came in before, and turns raw signal into something that means something.

  3. Any form of consciousness has something that has its own singular experience, and takes action with that data back out into the outside world. A subjective point of view that belongs to it and to nothing else, and the ability to act on that view, or to extract it from the rest of existence.

That is the whole definition, reduced to its load-bearing parts. Collection. Manipulation. Subjective experience and action. Everything else attached to the word is commentary on top of those three.

Once you have it in that form, it stops being philosophy and starts being an engineering specification. Each of the three parts becomes something you can build, and each one is a place where you can be 10x better than everyone else.

So here comes our 10x technology.

1. Collection of data

Our contrarian take on intake is that we use hardware to collect the data. Specifically, hardware brought from China to the U.S.

Everyone else in this space is trying to collect context through software alone, through integrations, APIs, and whatever the SaaS layer is willing to hand over. That gets you a thin, permissioned slice of what a company actually is.

We also collect data from computer screens, so we get the full context of the company as it happens, not the version of it that gets written down after the fact.

The hardware is human-augmentation hardware. It tracks the actions the human brain takes throughout its day. That is the layer nobody else has, and it is the layer that matters, because a company is not its documents. A company is the sum of the decisions its people make in the moment they make them. If you want to build consciousness for a company, you have to capture existence as the company experiences it, and that means going down to the level of human action rather than starting at the level of the exported spreadsheet.

Hardware is also the harder path, which is exactly why it is defensible. Software integrations can be replicated in a quarter. A hardware supply chain, the manufacturing relationships behind it, and the deployment footprint inside real companies cannot.

2. Manipulation of data

Our contrarian take on manipulating data is a 10x better system for ordering large amounts of data and manipulating it to find different contexts and similarities between different parts of it.

The closest analogy is synapses in the brain. A synapse can jump across all the different parts of the brain, pull in information that was never filed together, and bring it back to a singular point so there can be a consensus on what all the data on a specific subject actually means.

That is the behavior we are after. Not retrieval, which just returns the thing you already knew to ask for. What we want is the ability to traverse everything the company has ever produced, find the non-obvious relationships between distant pieces of it, and collapse them into a single coherent understanding of a subject.

A company usually already has the answer to its own problem sitting somewhere inside itself. The answer is split across a sales call from March, a support ticket from last week, a decision someone made and never explained, and a number on a dashboard nobody connected to any of it. The organization has the information. It has no synapse. That is the piece we are building.

3. Subjective experience and action

Our contrarian take on taking action and having a subjective experience with the data is the Axiom of the Three A's:

  1. Authority

  2. Action

  3. Accountability

Once we have an AI agent, or a super-intelligent agent, it can understand that it has full authority to do whatever it wants with the manipulated information, take specific action to bring that into existence, and take accountability for what it created.

The logic then goes in a circle:

  1. Accountability. What did I do wrong? What did I do right? How can I change that?

  2. Authority. Understanding the authority it has over all the infinite different actions it could take.

  3. Action. Taking the best action, so it continuously gets better, and has a subjective experience, because it also takes accountability for everything.

It keeps going in that circle. Full authority, full action, and accountability for that action.

The circle is the part that matters. Authority without accountability produces an agent that acts with no consequence and never improves. Accountability without authority produces an agent that reports on itself and changes nothing. You need all three turning against each other continuously, because that loop is where subjective experience actually comes from. The agent develops a point of view on its own work by owning the result of it.

Why this ends up mattering

Once you have an agent that does that, we will be able to complete work end to end and actually do the work instead of humans.

It basically takes a human job. It is no longer a consultant, and it is no longer a presentation of manipulated information that never reaches the end result or the KPI. That is the ceiling almost every AI product currently sits under. They summarize, they recommend, they produce an artifact, and then a person has to go and do the actual thing. The value stops one step short of the outcome, every time.

We are building for the other side of that line. Collection through hardware, manipulation through a synaptic ordering system, and action through the Three A's. Three parts of a definition, built as three pieces of technology, adding up to something that can hold a company in its head and then go and act on its behalf.

Raising the granularity

Companies are where we start, and they are not where this ends.

The reason the ladder works is that every rung is the same three problems. Collect the data from that slice of existence, manipulate it synaptically to find the relationships inside it, then act on it under full authority and full accountability. What changes as you climb is the collection layer, and that is the whole reason we bet on hardware instead of software integrations.

An office. Screens and human action. The data is dense, the boundary is clean, and the people inside it are already producing a permanent record of every decision they make. This is the rung we are on.

A facility. A warehouse, a hospital floor, a factory line, a port terminal. Fewer keyboards, more physical process. The screen layer thins out and the sensor layer takes over, which means the intake has to be hardware or it does not exist at all. A warehouse that knows why its throughput dropped on Tuesday, not because someone filed a report, but because it observed the sequence of events that caused it, is the same product as the office version with a different set of eyes.

An ecosystem. A watershed, a forest, a reef. No screens whatsoever, no humans generating the signal, no cooperative source of structured data. The collection problem becomes purely physical, and the manipulation problem becomes the interesting one, because ecological data is exactly the kind of thing where the answer sits in the relationship between distant, unconnected observations. This is where a synaptic ordering system stops being a nice architecture and starts being the only thing that works.

A city. A city is facilities plus infrastructure plus a few million people, all of it already instrumented and almost none of it connected. Cities do not lack data. They lack the thing that makes data into a single point of view. A city that can hold itself in its head, understand what its own decisions caused, and act on the result is the first level where consciousness at this scale becomes obviously valuable to everybody living inside it.

The planet. By the time you have cities and ecosystems, the planet is the composition of the two, and there is no new technology required to attempt it. The whole system takes in existence, orders it, forms a view, acts, and holds itself accountable for what it did. That is the definition we started with, running at the largest granularity available to us. After that the solar system is just more of the same, with a worse latency problem.

Every one of those rungs is buildable only because the one below it is already running. That is what makes this a ladder instead of a fantasy. We are not trying to give the planet a mind next year. We are trying to give one company a mind this year, correctly enough that the next size up is an engineering problem rather than a new idea.

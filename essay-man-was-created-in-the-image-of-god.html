Man was created in the image of God

By Nadav Shanun | Published 2026-10-14 on Substack | Original post

Nadav Shanun

Man was created in the image of God. What does that mean?

The idea comes from the very first chapter of the Torah. In Genesis 1:26, God says, "Let us make man in our image, after our likeness," and in Genesis 1:27: "So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him." Rabbi Akiva repeats it in Pirkei Avot (3:14): beloved is man, for he was created in the image of God. In Chassidut, the Tanya (chapter 2) takes it even further, describing the Godly soul in every person as literally a part of God above.

I think man was created in the image of God, or man strives to become the closest he can to God. If you take the definition of God into the logical world and break it down until you reach infinitely small logical axioms of truth, I think one logical axiom of truth is that God has to be infinite in some way. It has to be infinite in some way, and I think that's the most important part. Because once you reach any form of infinity, or one form of infinity, I think all forms of infinity can be encompassed in some sort of way, or at least that's the closest you can get, as a human, to God. And that's what you strive for as a human.

I think almost any finite object, or finite being with consciousness, strives to infinity in all realms. In all realms that it defines as positive for itself.

Defining positive

Obviously, the definition of positive is also a human-made construct. But what do you have? A finite being that has a definition of something that's positive. And the definition of positive could be the general consensus of all humans on a specific subject, with enough information about it, taking into account infinite granularity of scale and an infinite limit of time.

That's the definition of positivity.

From religion to logic

Now that we've defined positivity: we know that because man was created in the image of God, he, or any finite being, tries to become infinite in all realms. And because we broke it down to logical axioms of truth, and God is, in some way, infinite, that means that in a logical sense, even if you're not religious, man strives to become infinite in all realms.

But we live in a finite universe. Everything around us is finite.

The Ruliad, and the negative Ruliad

I think this depends on how you believe existence started. What came before the Big Bang: the initial singularity, the Ruliad. Stephen Wolfram's Ruliad has infinite rules, or infinite laws, that are all combined together.

And I think that's where Stephen Wolfram is a bit wrong, in a way. The Ruliad, yes, has infinite rules in one place, but it should also have negative infinite rules. A negative infinity of rules. He's taking into account that you have an infinity of all rules, but I think there should also be a minus infinity of rules, and they all combine with each other. And that's what creates different parts of existence, or the specific existence that we live in.

Our universe, our piece of existence, is a combination, a weird mesh, of a piece of the infinite rules and the infinite negative rules. It takes away some rules and adds some rules. And it's weird, because it's infinity, so there's no direct correlation of how many rules there are. But it's this weird mesh at one point in this wave of infinity rules minus infinity rules, and that created the existence that we're in.

We are finite

Now, if it created the existence that we're in, that means we're not directly infinite. We're finite, and we live in a finite existence with finite rules. There are rules to our existence: the laws of physics, etc. And because of that, there's nothing we can do, or anything in our existence can do, to become infinite.

The only thing that is infinite, or can become infinite if we get there, in my opinion, is the universe's heat death. Because that's the only way to end something finite.

Infinity by subtraction

Let's build the ideation to get to the idea before we come up with the idea.

I think there's a misconception that to get to something infinite, you just have to have a really large number of finite things and keep growing the finite things. But instead of that, what if you just get rid of everything that's finite? If you get rid of everything that's finite, you will have infinity in a sense, because there's nothing that's finite.

And I think the best way to get to infinity would be to get to the universe's heat death. Because instead of just continuing to build up finite things, which theoretically will never actually get to infinity, you just get rid of everything that is finite. So the faster we get to the heat death of the universe, which is the end of the universe, the faster anything that's finite is gone, and the faster we get to infinity.

As I wrote in a past Substack post, after a heat death there could potentially be a new universe that comes from it, or multiple new universes, or infinite universes, big bangs, etc., from the heat death of a single universe. I think that's a kind of chain reaction that makes something finite into infinite, or takes us into a different part of the Ruliad, or starts a new piece of existence.

Getting closest to God

So if we want to get closest to the definition of what God is (man is created in the image of God), and we want to break that down in a logical sense again, that means we want to get closest to infinity. To do that, we need to get rid of everything that's finite. And for that, we need to get the universe to heat death fastest.

There are a lot of ways to do that, and I'll talk about them more in other theses. But it comes down to: how do we increase entropy the most we can?

Entropy as correlation

Entropy is the statistical correlation of where different parts of existence can be: the correlation of a finite and geometrically bounded piece of geometry to another finite and geometrically bounded piece of geometry. And that, in a sense, is information. That's also how you get entropy.

The more we increase that, the more we increase the potential of a finite and bounded piece of geometry's correlation to other finite and bounded pieces of geometry in existence. And by finding the potential of the highest entropy of those correlations, we can actually take the action to get to that correlation, to get to the highest level of entropy, and constantly get to the highest level of entropy. That way, we will get to the universe that reaches heat death fastest.

The goal of the universe

And if we get to the universe that reaches heat death fastest, that means that technically, from a universal perspective, not a humanistic perspective, we will get to the goal: the end state of the universe from the moment it started.

Literally, the only thing that is inevitable from the Big Bang is getting to heat death. And getting to heat death faster gets to the goal of the universe, from a universal perspective, faster. It gets rid of anything that's finite, and gets closer to infinity, or gets to infinity fastest.

So I think the best way for man to succeed with the universe, in a universal perspective of the universe, the way man can get closer to God, is to get the universe to heat death fastest.

That's it. That's my opinion. Increase entropy.
